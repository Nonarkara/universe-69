## 1. John B. Calhoun's "Universe 25" (NIMH, 1968–1973)

**Setup.** Universe 25 was the culminating "mouse utopia" built by ethologist John B. Calhoun at the National Institute of Mental Health. The enclosure was a square tank 101 inches (about 2.57 m) on a side with 54-inch-high walls — climbable mesh on the lower portion, unclimbable above, open-topped [1][2]. Each wall carried 16 vertical mesh tunnels ("stairwells"), each tunnel serving 4 horizontal corridors, each corridor opening onto 4 nesting boxes: 256 apartments in total, each able to house about 15 mice, for a nominal nesting capacity of roughly 3,840 [2][3]. Food hoppers and water dispensers were effectively unlimited, nesting paper was abundant, the climate was held comfortable, predators were absent, and disease was minimized (though the pen was cleaned only every four to eight weeks, a point critics return to) [1][2][3]. The only limited resource was space, and more precisely socially meaningful space and roles [4][5].

**Founding and the four phases.** On 9 July 1968 Calhoun introduced four breeding pairs — eight BALB/c albino mice from NIH's disease-free colony [6][7]. In his 1973 paper "Death Squared: The Explosive Growth and Demise of a Mouse Population" he divided the colony's history into phases [1][6]:

- **Phase A ("strive")** — roughly the first 104 days: adjustment, territory formation, first litters.
- **Phase B ("exploit")** — rapid growth, population doubling every 55 days, reaching 600+ mice by **day 315** [2][6]. Growth was already unevenly distributed: some nesting areas were far more crowded than others.
- **Phase C ("stagnation")** — from day 315 to about day 560: doubling time slowed to roughly 145 days while social organization broke down [2][6]. All territorial/social roles were filled; surplus males, with nowhere to emigrate (Calhoun stressed that in nature losers disperse, which the sealed universe forbade), either withdrew into inactive huddles — covered in wounds from sporadic attacks they didn't resist — or became hyperaggressive. Violence, cannibalism, and "pansexual" mounting became endemic; nursing females turned aggressive, abandoned or attacked pups, and reproduction began failing [2][3][8].
- **Phase D ("death")** — the population peaked at about **2,200 around day 560** (some retellings round this to "the 19th month" or "about 2,200"; Calhoun's own figure is 2,200) [1][2][3]. After day 600 virtually no pups survived; the last conception is usually placed around **day 920** [6]. By 22 June 1972 only 122 mice remained, past reproductive age, and the colony was counted as functionally extinct, dying out fully by 1973 [8][2].

**Behavioral sink and "the beautiful ones."** The *behavioral sink* — a term Calhoun coined in his 1962 *Scientific American* rat studies — describes pathological, self-reinforcing crowding: animals learned to associate eating with company and piled into a few hoppers while others sat unused, so pathology spread "like a contagion" even though resources never ran short [7][3]. The signature Universe 25 cohort was the "beautiful ones": males raised amid the breakdown who never fought, never courted, never mated — they only ate, slept, and groomed, leaving sleek, unscarred coats, "wrapped in narcissistic introspection" [2][3]. Their female counterparts likewise withdrew to high nesting boxes and did not breed.

**Why no recovery.** The common intuition — density falls, breeding resumes — did not hold. Mice reared during Phases C–D never acquired courtship, maternal, or territorial behaviors; Calhoun called them "trapped in an infantile state" [8]. When he transplanted small groups of beautiful ones to fresh, empty universes, they still failed to breed normally. His conclusion was that a **"first death" — death of the spirit (social death, Ds)** — precedes and guarantees the **"second death"** of the body: once all social roles are filled and the young cannot practice meaningful roles, the capacity for species-typical behavior is lost even if space later reopens. He framed this as "death squared," with an epigraph from Revelation [1][6][4].

**Modern critiques.** (a) *Replication:* two quasi-replications (Kessler 1966; Hammock 1971) largely failed to reproduce the headline collapse, and no one has replicated Universe 25 [7][9]. (b) *Genetic confound:* BALB/c is a highly inbred laboratory strain, and five years of breeding inside a closed universe founded by 8 animals compounds inbreeding — behavioral degeneration cannot be cleanly attributed to density [9]. (c) *Husbandry confound:* infrequent cleaning means disease and parasitism were plausible co-drivers [7]. (d) *Design, not density:* Ramsden and Adams report Calhoun's own view that the pathology tracked the **distribution** of space, resources, and social roles — the architecture funneled animals into contact — and that Calhoun's own later work (e.g., rewarding cooperation, "ecologically enriched" universes) showed outcomes were design-dependent, not an iron law of crowding [4][5]. (e) *Over-extrapolation:* the 1970s urban-doom readings were political projection; Jonathan Freedman's human studies (1975) found no appreciable pathology from density per se, and pointed to poverty and inequality instead; "excessive uncontrolled social interaction" was Calhoun's framing [7][4][3].

## 2. *Love, Death & Robots*, Vol. 1, Ep. 16 — "Ice Age" (2019)

"Ice Age" is episode 16 of Netflix's anthology (released 15 March 2019), directed by series co-creator Tim Miller, based on a short story by Michael Swanwick, and starring Topher Grace (Rob) and Mary Elizabeth Winstead (Gail) [10][11]. It is the season's lone live-action episode (actors plus CG freezer interiors) [11][12].

**Plot.** A young couple moving into an old apartment inherit an antique refrigerator. Defrosting the freezer, they find a woolly mammoth frozen in the frost — then tiny hunters: an ice-age micro-civilization living at hyper-accelerated time. Each time they close and reopen the door, centuries have passed inside. Observed stages: ice-age megafauna and hunters → a medieval castle society → an industrial/early-modern city → a sprawling modern metropolis → full-scale nuclear war that devastates the micro-world → a post-war leap into a hyper-advanced, post-singularity civilization whose inhabitants transcend into luminous energy forms and depart/vanish, leaving the ice empty [11][12]. The ending is cyclical: by morning, primitive life has reappeared in the frost — a new civilization beginning the climb again [12].

**Core ideas for simulation design:** (1) *time-compressed civilizational observation* — a bounded micro-world whose history runs orders of magnitude faster than the observers' time; (2) *non-intervening observers* — the couple watch, worry, and refrain from interfering, gods only in viewpoint; (3) *cyclicality* — collapse (nuclear war) is not terminal; transcendence/vacancy is followed by reseeding, so the universe is a loop rather than an arc [11][12].

## 3. Harari, *Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind*

Harari (2011 Hebrew; 2014 English) organizes human history around four revolutions: Cognitive (~70,000 years ago), Agricultural (~12,000 years ago), the Unification of Humankind, and the Scientific Revolution (~1543 CE onward) [13].

**Cognitive revolution.** What made *Homo sapiens* dominant was the ability to "cooperate flexibly in large numbers," enabled by language that can describe **fictions** — gods, nations, money, corporations, rights — that exist only in shared imagination [13]. Two sub-claims matter for simulation: the **gossip theory** (language evolved substantially to share social information about third parties, which sustains trust in bands) and the **Dunbar-style ~150 threshold**: gossip alone stabilizes groups only up to roughly 150 individuals; beyond that, cooperation requires shared myths. Groups without common fictions should therefore fission at ~150 [13][14].

**Agricultural revolution as "history's biggest fraud."** Farming (from ~10,000 BCE) raised total food energy and **population**, but made the average individual worse off: a narrower, less reliable diet; longer and harder labor (bodies adapted to foraging, not fields); new diseases from settlement and density; vulnerability to single-crop failure; and the birth of hierarchy, property, and elites extracting surplus [13][14]. Harari's inversion — "we did not domesticate wheat, wheat domesticated us" — captures the mechanism: each incremental step (more food → more children → more mouths → more farming) was locally rational and collectively irreversible. He generalizes this as the **luxury trap**: luxuries become necessities and generate new obligations; there is no going back because the enlarged population can no longer be fed by foraging [13][14].

**Unification and science.** Money, empires, and universal religions are the three great unifiers converting many small human worlds into one interdependent one [13]. The Scientific Revolution is distinguished by the institutional **admission of ignorance** ("we do not know") coupled with capital and empire. Critics (Strawson; Narayanan) fault the book for sensationalism and for storytelling outrunning evidence — worth remembering when treating its claims as parameters rather than hypotheses [13].

**Most relevant to a forager→farmer transition:** foragers = varied diet, mobility, low disease, fewer working hours, hard cap on group size without lore; adopting agriculture = carrying capacity rises sharply while per-capita welfare (diet quality, health, leisure) falls, settlement locks in, hierarchy and specialists emerge, and reversal becomes impossible once population overshoots the foraging baseline [13][14].

## 4. Diamond, *Guns, Germs, and Steel* (1997)

**Yali's question.** The book opens with New Guinean politician Yali asking why whites "developed so much cargo" — why material power is so unevenly distributed. Diamond's answer: not biology but **geography and biogeography**; guns, germs, and steel are proximate causes, environment the ultimate one [15].

**Domestication and the Anna Karenina principle.** A species is domesticable only if it passes *every* test (diet, growth rate, captive breeding, disposition, panic response, social hierarchy) — failure on any one disqualifies it. Of ~148 large terrestrial herbivorous mammal candidates, only **14** were ever domesticated, and the "major five" (cattle, sheep, goat, pig, horse) are all of Eurasian origin; the Americas had essentially the llama/alpaca, Australia none [15]. Plant domesticates were similarly concentrated in the Fertile Crescent's large-seeded grasses.

**Axes.** Eurasia's **east–west axis** let crops, livestock, technologies, and ideas diffuse along similar latitudes and day-lengths; the **north–south axes** of the Americas and Africa forced diffusion across climate barriers, slowing or blocking it [15].

**The causal chain.** Food production → storable surplus → sedentism and higher population density → non-food specialists (soldiers, smiths, scribes, priests) → technology, writing, and centralized states [15]. This chain is directly implementable as a tech/population ladder.

**Germs.** The major epidemic "crowd diseases" — smallpox, measles, influenza — evolved from pathogens of domesticated herd animals living at close quarters with dense human populations. Eurasians acquired partial immunity; isolated populations did not. Imported disease, more than combat, collapsed Native American populations (often ~90%+ regional die-offs), making conquest possible [15].

**Critiques.** The book won the 1998 Pulitzer but is widely criticized for **environmental determinism** that minimizes human agency, institutions, and contingency (Acemoğlu & Robinson's institutional counter-argument; Blaut's charge that it re-launders Eurocentrism); for **cherry-picking** cases and oversimplifying (e.g., treating "Eurasia" as a unit, underplaying why Europe rather than China); and for errors of detail flagged by historians and anthropologists [15]. Use its mechanisms (domesticates, axes, germs, surplus chain) as simulation levers, not as settled historiography.

## 5. Design implications for a dual-universe simulation

| Verified finding | Universe A mechanic (Calhoun-style utopia) | Universe B mechanic (primitive ecology) |
|---|---|---|
| Unlimited food/water, ideal climate, no predators, no disease, bounded space; nominal capacity 3,840 never reached — the peak was ~57% of it (~2,200); stagnation began near 620, about 16% [1][2][3] | Provision food/water as infinite; cap *space and social roles*, not calories; expect collapse well below nominal capacity | Food is the binding constraint: foraging/fishing/hunting yields vary by season and patch; predation and starvation are live death causes |
| Phase structure: strive (A) → exploit (B, double/55 days to day 315) → stagnate (C, double/145 days) → death (D, peak ~2,200 at ~day 560; last conception ~day 920) [1][2][6] | Hard-code phase detection from growth-rate inflections; growth slows when social roles saturate, long before space does | Growth tracks carrying capacity of the ecology; no role-saturation collapse — groups fission and migrate instead |
| Behavioral sink: pathology clusters where design concentrates contact; distribution of space/roles, not raw density, drives collapse [4][5] | Model per-location congestion; learned "eat together" habits create hotspots; pathology is contagious by social learning | Spatial dispersal is always available; emigration is the escape valve Calhoun's sealed box removed |
| Beautiful ones: non-breeding, non-fighting groomers; withdrawn wounded males; maternal neglect; violence [2][3][8] | Agents raised during stagnation permanently lack courtship/parenting behaviors (no re-learning); add non-breeder, hyperaggressor, and neglectful-mother states | Parenting/courtship transmitted by normal socialization; failure modes are external (death of parents, famine), not role-loss |
| First death precedes second death: no recovery even when density falls; transplants failed to breed [1][4] | Represent loss of reproductive competence as a candidate mechanism; measure whether each run empties rather than assuming an inevitable endpoint | Recovery may remain possible after a crash when a viable remnant and resources persist; test the outcome in each run |
| Critiques: inbreeding, disease confounds, failed replications [7][9] | Optionally expose toggles (genetic diversity, hygiene) so the collapse is a scenario, not destiny | Genetic diversity and zoonotic disease are explicit variables from the start |
| Ice Age: time-compressed observation, non-intervening gods, cyclic reset [11][12] | Observer UI: open-the-freezer sampling of a fast-running world; no intervention controls | Same observer frame; on extinction or transcendence, reseed a primitive population (cyclical universe) |
| Sapiens: fiction/lore enables cooperation past ~150; gossip stabilizes small bands [13][14] | N/A (mice have no lore — which is precisely why roles can't be reorganized) | Groups without shared lore fission at ~150; a "language/lore" tech unlocks larger stable settlements |
| Agriculture = biggest fraud / luxury trap: population up, welfare down, irreversible [13][14] | N/A | Agriculture raises carrying capacity sharply but lowers diet quality/health, raises workload and disease; once population overshoots, reverting to foraging causes famine (one-way door) |
| GGS: Anna Karenina domestication filter; ~14 of 148 mammals; surplus→specialists→states; crowd diseases from livestock; axes [15] | N/A | Tech ladder: fire → tools → fishing → language/lore → agriculture → domestication (only if suitable species spawn locally) → epidemics (triggered by livestock+density) → villages → metallurgy; map geometry affects diffusion speed |

The experiments contrast provisioned material security with social-role constraints against a resource-limited world that can acquire technology. Persistence, recovery and collapse are outcomes to measure in versioned runs. This contrast does not establish a law of human social decline; the proposed agriculture trade-off also remains a model hypothesis.

## 6. Slingerland, *Drunk: How We Sipped, Danced, and Stumbled Our Way to Civilization* (2021)

Intoxication as a **technology for binding people** — and the book that makes
the model's night watch legible.

**Intoxication suppresses prefrontal control.** Alcohol lowers executive function,
and a less-controlled person is *more* trusted and more open. In laboratory
settings (Sayette 2012), alcohol raises group-level positive affect and
self-reported bonding, and the effect is pharmacological, not expectancy.
Smiles spread further in drinking groups, with the contagion strongest in
male-dominated groups [16].

**The night watch as group vigilance.** Drinking in a group protects against
the risk alcohol creates: the group watches one another's backs, and a solitary
drinker is the one in trouble. The model's night watch — one adult per camp
standing guard while the band sleeps — uses this group-vigilance analogy: the
turn taken on watch is what makes the night safe for everyone else. The book
says the group moderates the drinker, not that it watches for predators; the
analogy is a model choice, not a measured result [16].

**Violence and intoxication.** Alcohol is the only drug but pure stimulants that
reliably *increases* physical aggression, and it does so in those already
predisposed. A low-self-control band that gathers is a band whose fights are
worse. Distillation is a quantum leap in alcohol content: 9,000 years of weak
beer, then brandy — a technology with a threshold changes the world, not just
the drinker [16].

**Caveat.** Laboratory findings on social drinkers, not ethnography. The
pharmacological bonding effect is one study (Sayette 2012). The night-watch
analogy is a model choice.

## 7. Malinowski, *Argonauts of the Western Pacific* (1922)

The founding text of how a working anthropology actually looks at work, magic
and obligation. The Trobriand Islanders are the people studied.

**The kula.** A long-distance ceremonial exchange: shell necklaces travel one
way, armbands the other, forever. The gifts are not useful; the obligation is
the point. A `kula` mechanic would have bands exchange a surplus good in
opposite directions, and the exchange itself raises the standing of both. Not
on the tech ladder yet — a candidate for future work. The kula gives the
tribes a reason to stay in contact rather than only to fission.

**Magic as practical response.** Gardening magic, war magic, healing magic —
each answers a real worry with a ritual. Every disaster the model already has
gets a myth; magic is the *response* to a fear, not a cause. The model has
the response (myths are recorded on disasters), not the worry.

**Dual principles.** The same people hold two principles at once — the
*competitive* and the *tolerant* — and switch between them by context. A band
is competitive with strangers and tolerant inside. The violence model needs
both, not one.

**Division of labour and apprenticeship.** Crafts and trades emerge from what
a band must have, not from a tech list. Skill is taught because the exchange
needs it.

**The observer's stance.** The ethnographer works among people who do not know
they are being studied, and the observer's "rational" explanations are often
theirs too. A model's hypotheses should be testable by the world — which is
what the RESEARCH panel now does.

**Caveat.** Ethnography of one society (Trobriand Islanders), early 20th
century. The kula is not implemented; it is a candidate mechanic.

## 8. Dower, *War without Mercy: Race and Power in the Pacific War* (1986)

The violence chapter's missing half: how a group comes to treat people as
things.

**Dehumanisation is a mechanism, not a metaphor.** It widens the psychological
distance that makes killing possible, and it reaches the planners, not only
the soldiers. Being treated as *the other* lowers the threshold for lethal
violence, for the band as well as for the observer.

**Nonhuman metaphors.** Vermin, insects, herd — these become routine idioms,
and once routine they are hard to dislodge. A name that turns into a category:
once a kind of person is a *kind*, the violence against them becomes
administration. This applies to both worlds: in EDEN, the placeless become
"surplus"; in LAGOON, a tribe that loses a feud becomes "the other".

**Atrocity and hate feed each other.** A feud that escalates is not a sum of
grievances but a loop; the grudge decay rate may be too kind. The model's
grudge decays ~8%/yr — Dower suggests the fuse is longer and the resurfacing
is delayed.

**Burial of memory.** Most combatants who survived chose not to remember, and
buried it; the past resurfaces only years later. Unresolved violence has a long
fuse. The model's `grudge` and `gd` (grudge held against a person) capture
this partially but do not model the delayed resurfacing.

**Caveat.** War, not a forager band. The dehumanisation mechanic is a
candidate for future work — a kind of person that the record turns into a
category, so that killing them becomes routine. It needs calibration in
`tools/lab.py` before it goes near `DESIGN.md`.

## Corrections from the October 2026 reading of the primaries

- Ramsden & Adams are historians: they *report* Calhoun's own view that design, not density, drove the pathology; they do not test it. Their main argument is that popular retellings reduced the work to "crowding causes madness" and ignored Calhoun's cure: more rooms, controlled contact, and the "creative deviants" among the withdrawn [4].
- The "excessive uncontrolled social interaction" thesis is Calhoun's. Freedman's critique blamed poverty and inequality, not contact [4].
- In the Towson rat pen, the population expected to reach 5,000 levelled at 150 and never passed 200. Infant mortality reached 96% in sink groups (Calhoun 1962, *Scientific American*).
- The day-level milestones (days 104, 315, 560, 920) are still from a secondary source [6]; the primary *Death Squared* text could not be fetched this round.

## Tropical island analogue — 2026-10-30

LAGOON uses a **Sri Lanka-inspired coastal ecology**, not a surveyed copy of
Sri Lanka or a promise that sixteen founders will establish a civilization.
FAO describes coastal lowlands, uplands, monsoon rainfall and freshwater
fisheries; the implementation uses wet and dry tropical seasons rather than
European winters for newly founded worlds. [FAO, *Reservoirs of Sri Lanka and
their fisheries*, chapter 2](https://www.fao.org/4/T0028E/T0028E02.htm).

WWCT's field study at Ritigala records forest mammals and deer prey, and
explains their relationship to leopard habitat. The model's deer and big cats
are abstract populations, not an exhaustive fauna census. [WWCT, Ritigala
Strict Natural Reserve study](https://www.wwct.org/study-sites/ritigala-snr).

These sources support habitat relationships, not the simulation's numerical
coefficients. Patch yields, freshwater reserves, thirst thresholds, fertility
and discovery probabilities remain illustrative model choices. Freshwater
banks are finite shared resources; ocean and lagoon water are not drinking
sources. There is no complete hydrological, nutritional or pathogen model.
Heritable traits are not a genome: kinship genetics and inbreeding depression
are not simulated. The current technology ladder ends at writing; it does
not predict an industrial or modern civilization's arrival date.


### Milk supplies water as well as food

The model must credit water whenever it credits nursing food. WHO explains
that breast milk contains water and supports continued breastfeeding beyond
the first year. [WHO breastfeeding Q&A](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/breastfeeding).
The implementation keeps its existing under-three nursing/following window
for both flows, with maternal cost and proximity; that cutoff and the units
are model calibration, not a derived clinical feeding schedule.

## Personal resource experience — primary human evidence (2026-10-30)

Three human experiments support qualitative, experience-sensitive resource
search:

- **Remembering food locations.** de Vries et al. (2020) covertly tested
  location memory after people followed a route through food or food-odour
  stations. Participants incidentally learned locations, with better recall
  for high-calorie stimuli. This supports resource-location memory; an
  ancestral adaptation remains an interpretation. [*Scientific Reports*
  10:15174](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-72570-x).
- **Search responds to encounters.** Hills, Kalff and Wiener (2013) tracked
  human search for hidden resources in a virtual environment. Resource
  encounters triggered more intensive local searching in patchy environments;
  the dispersed condition differed. This supports context-sensitive search,
  rather than a universal movement rule. [*PLOS ONE* 8(4):e60488](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0060488).
- **Learning sampled rewards.** Wu et al. (2018) studied human search in
  spatially correlated bandit tasks with up to 121 options. Their results
  supported learning and generalization from observed rewards to guide
  exploration. Their fitted Gaussian-process model is not a required
  implementation for this simulation. [*Nature Human Behaviour*
  2:915–924](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-018-0467-4).

**Candidate mapping, not human calibration.** Give a person a bounded record
of places they actually visited and outcomes they actually obtained. Let
successful and empty attempts adjust future preferences among accessible
sites, alongside travel cost and continued exploration. Keep observations
local: an unvisited site is unknown, and a remembered success does not reveal
its current stock. Memory capacity, forgetting, exploration probability,
update rates and any practice multiplier are illustrative design choices.
These papers do not supply those coefficients or establish harvesting-skill
growth rates.

**Limits.** These are modern experimental tasks, not longitudinal studies
of Palaeolithic survival or small isolated populations. They do not validate
LAGOON's demography, resource regeneration, freshwater memory, inherited
knowledge, or a claim that memory will prevent extinction. Seasonal change
and depletion can make old experience misleading. Any population effect
must be reported as a result of the versioned model, not a measured human
benefit.


## Sharing resource knowledge through contact — primary human evidence (2026-10-30)

King et al. (2011) compared communicating and non-communicating human groups
in a physical foraging arena with patches of differing quality. Communicating
groups collected resources more effectively and reached consensus on the
profitable patch more often. Gesture frequency was associated with consensus;
the experiment did not isolate a universal gesture-learning coefficient.
[*Biology Letters* 7:237–240, doi:10.1098/rsbl.2010.0808](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3061177/).

Information sharing is not uniformly beneficial. In Deffner et al.'s (2024)
human immersive-reality resource-search experiment, extensive social
information use in concentrated environments was associated with individual
gains and collective losses; incentives changed how selectively people used
others' information. [*Nature Communications* 15:2683,
doi:10.1038/s41467-024-47010-3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-47010-3).

**Candidate mapping.** Permit a recorded encounter to pass a bounded piece
of the sender's acquired resource knowledge. A report is evidence from
another person, not a fresh measurement of the site's current stock. Keep
personal outcomes and socially received reports distinguishable; retain
independent exploration and the possibility of stale information or crowding.
Neither study supports knowledge appearing automatically across a whole
population without a transmission opportunity.

**Limits.** These tasks support qualitative information use and coordination,
not this model's contact radius, transfer rate, trust weight, memory capacity
or forgetting rate. They do not establish historical language requirements,
parent-to-child inheritance, long-term knowledge fidelity, or an extinction
benefit in isolated human populations. Any such rules and coefficients remain
explicit model choices; a local encounter gate is an operational simplification,
not a measured prehistoric communication range.

## Procedural skill and cumulative culture — primary human evidence (2026-10-30)

Morgan et al. (2015) compared transmission conditions while modern adults
learned to make Oldowan-style stone flakes. Teaching, especially verbal
teaching, improved several measures of actual production relative to simpler
transmission conditions. Learners performed the knapping task: this was not
a test of instant proficiency from hearing a technology's name. Performance
also deteriorated along transmission chains, so communication did not ensure
perfect retention or continuing improvement. [*Nature Communications*
6:6029, doi:10.1038/ncomms7029](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms7029).

The successful learning route depends on the task. Caldwell and Millen (2009)
found cumulative improvement in paper-airplane construction under imitation,
emulation and teaching conditions independently. Observing actions or having
a teacher was not universally necessary: learners could use end products and
outcomes. [*Psychological Science* 20:1478–1483,
doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02469.x](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02469.x).

**Candidate constraints before tuning.** Keep exposure to a technique separate
from demonstrated ability to perform it. Let instruction or demonstration
supply useful information, while actual attempts and their outcomes provide
practice evidence. Do not turn an incidental conversation into instant
mastery, or assume every contact improves performance. Equally, do not claim
a universal requirement for spoken language, direct imitation or a living
teacher; artifact-based learning is another possible route when artifacts
are represented. Distinguish discovery, transmission, individual proficiency
and successful collective use. Loss and imperfect transmission remain
possible; cultural accumulation is an outcome to test.

**Limits.** These are particular modern-adult tasks, not calibrated histories
of prehistoric apprenticeship or every technology in LAGOON. They do not
supply proficiency growth rates, mastery thresholds, childhood schedules,
required teacher counts, forgetting constants or survival multipliers.
Those are explicit model choices. The tool-making experiment informs a
hypothesis about teaching and language evolution; it does not establish the
historical sequence or validate the simulation's technological ladder.


**Implementation distinction (engine 2026.10.30.5).** The existing task evidence
supports separating instruction from real practice; it does not establish that
prehistoric people used this model's prerequisite graph, mentor list, request
utility, age gate, sight radius, attention budget or search deadline. Purposeful
seeking is an explicit candidate mechanism. A fishing observer may ask the
actual expert and receive foundational tool instruction through a voluntary
conversation; neither seeing fishing nor asking can grant tool competence.
Failure to locate or engage a teacher remains possible. Interpret measured
transmission under this mechanism separately from artifact learning, incidental
conversation and practical production. One successful controlled lesson does
not establish population-level cultural accumulation or a survival benefit.


## Reproductive encounters and observable labour — causal limits (2026-10-30)

Wilcox, Weinberg and Baird (1995) followed women trying to conceive using daily
intercourse records and urinary hormone measurements. In 625 analyzable cycles,
conceptions were associated with intercourse in a six-day interval ending on
estimated ovulation. Exposure and reproductive timing matter; the study does
not calibrate an untimed population-level hazard or this model's per-part
coefficients. [*New England Journal of Medicine* 333(23):1517–1521,
doi:10.1056/NEJM199512073332301](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199512073332301).

**Candidate causal boundary.** Require a completed reproductive encounter
before a conception opportunity, rather than treating a social bond or a
nearby partner as intercourse. Completion is the model's operational event
boundary, not a biological duration established by this study. Record the
model's genetic father from the
encounter selected by that opportunity, independently of later partnerships.
This is simulation bookkeeping, not an empirical method of inferring human
paternity. Same-part conception attribution omits sperm persistence, ovulation,
the fertile window and competing exposures; a completed encounter is not a
biological guarantee of fertilization. Retaining existing coefficients while
changing the exposure gate still changes the trajectory and does not preserve
a calibrated birth rate. No fertile-window rule or fitted rate is introduced
by this citation.

WHO's *Labour care guide: user's manual* (2020), Annex 4, recommendations 5–6,
describes contractions and cervical change before birth and emphasizes that
latent-stage duration varies. It supports representing labour before delivery,
not a universal one-day countdown. A visible early-labour interval of one model
day is an explicit illustrative timing choice, not a clinical estimate or a
care protocol. [WHO manual](https://www.who.int/publications/b/56119),
[official recommendation summary, nos. 5–6](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/maternal-health/ipc.pdf?sfvrsn=dc30fc00_8).

Kin recognition is another bounded approximation. Lieberman, Tooby and Cosmides
(2007) tested maternal association and childhood co-residence as cues related
to sibling-directed altruism and sexual aversion. This supports investigating
perceived kinship; it does not establish a universal categorical mating rule.
[*Nature* 445:727–731, doi:10.1038/nature05510](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature05510),
[primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3581061/).
The current recorded-parent/shared-parent candidate exclusion is an explicit
simplification, not a model of those perceptual cues, cultural norms, genetic
load or every person's behavior. Maternal identity and actual recorded paternal
identity are bookkeeping facts here. A future human-agency model must distinguish
perceived kinship from the observer's genealogy and test the resulting choices.


## Fertility probabilities: endpoint, cycle and exposure units (2026-10-30)

The Wilcox (1995) result above concerns urinary hCG-detected pregnancy around
implantation, not direct observation of fertilization. Its estimated
single-day exposure probabilities range from 0.10 at ovulation day −5 to 0.33
at day 0; neither is a per-part hazard. Clinical pregnancy and live birth are
different endpoints. [Original NEJM article](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199512073332301).

Li, Wilcox and Dunson (2015), §2.3, explicitly state a single-act **clinical
pregnancy** benchmark for days −5 through 0 relative to ovulation:
`[0.04, 0.13, 0.08, 0.29, 0.27, 0.08]`. Their outside-window zero is a modelling
approximation, not proof of biological impossibility. This is an endpoint-specific
benchmark, not a fitted fertilization parameter for LAGOON.
[*Contraception* 91:344–349, doi:10.1016/j.contraception.2015.01.002](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4374046/).

Dunson et al. (1999) corrected error in ovulation markers and found the highest
clinical-pregnancy estimate before ovulation; marker error changes the apparent
curve. [*Human Reproduction* 14:1835–1839,
doi:10.1093/humrep/14.7.1835](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10402400/).
Dunson, Colombo and Baird (2002) compared age groups in healthy natural-family-
planning couples; this does not supply a universal linear individual age curve.
[*Human Reproduction* 17:1399–1403,
doi:10.1093/humrep/17.5.1399](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/769710).
Ovulation also varies between cycles, including reported regular cycles; a
shared 28-day calendar with fixed day-14 ovulation is not an empirical fit.
[Wilcox et al., *BMJ* 2000;321:1259–1262](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC27529/).

**Smallest defensible structure, not a calibration claim.** Keep an individual
cycle/ovulation phase and a bounded set of completed, day-indexed reproductive
exposures; resolve at most one pregnancy outcome per cycle. Repeated animation
parts or one ongoing encounter cannot become independent daily exposures.
Any combination across exposed days needs an explicit dependence model.
Conditional independence in a viable cycle is a statistical assumption;
it does not license multiplying failures from marginal single-act probabilities.
[Sundaram et al., *Biostatistics* 2012;13:4–17,
doi:10.1093/biostatistics/kxr015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3276273/).

Cycle lengths, phase variation, initial phase, amenorrhoea, endpoint recognition,
multiple-partner attribution, within-cycle dependence and population modifiers
remain model choices unless separately estimated. An empirical clinical rate
cannot silently become a fertilization rate followed by another copy of losses
already included in that endpoint. No source here validates the current
per-part coefficient, a new linear fertile-day curve, or prehistoric survival.
A coarse cycle model may be tested as an explicit hypothesis; it must not be
called calibrated merely because one of its numbers appears in a paper.


**Selected coarse implementation (.7), with primary precedent and limits.**
Royston and Ferreira proposed an approximate most-fertile-intercourse-day
model, rather than requiring independent contributions from every intercourse
day. This supports investigating a best-exposure alternative; directly taking
the maximum of the 2015 marginal benchmark values is our own unfitted rule,
not their fitted algorithm. [*Biometrics* 1999;55:1005–1013,
doi:10.1111/j.0006-341x.1999.01005.x](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11315041/).
The chosen unit is one exposed day with at least one completed mutual contact;
additional parts or partners on that day do not add probability. Actual fathers
from the best day are retained, but choosing among them is a bookkeeping
assumption, not an estimated sperm-competition mechanism. Six daily bins
−5…0 do not mean a precisely measured 120-hour lifetime.

The sampled integer interval 24–35 days is illustrative, not a fitted menstrual
or ovulation generator. Bull et al. (2019), Table 1, report pooled ovulatory-cycle
length 29.3 ± 5.2 days and luteal length 12.4 ± 2.4 days (mean ± SD) in app data.
Those pooled summaries do not establish a uniform distribution or individual
cycle jitter; the sample excludes cycles without detected ovulation.
[*npj Digital Medicine* 2:83, doi:10.1038/s41746-019-0152-7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-019-0152-7).

Wilcox et al. (1988) distinguish hCG-detected losses before clinical recognition
from later recognized losses. Thus a clinical-pregnancy benchmark may be
followed by a separately stated later-loss model, without charging it again
for preclinical losses. The retained 0.14 probability and 50–90 days after the
model ovulation date are illustrative later-loss choices, not estimates fitted
from that paper. [*NEJM* 319:189–194,
doi:10.1056/NEJM198807283190401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3393170/).
